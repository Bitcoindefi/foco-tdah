EnFoco

Hi, I'm Leonardo Cagliero, founder of EnFoco.

EnFoco is a focus web app for adults with ADHD, and it does one thing: one task, one light on. You pick one thing, take a tiny first step, turn the light on, and keep it on while you do it.

No streaks, no guilt, and no data leaving your device.

2Slide 2 of 15

The problem

⏱ 25 sstarts at 0:20ends at 0:45

Open slide 2 →

Most people with ADHD know exactly what they need to do. The hard part is starting, and staying.

Barkley describes ADHD as a problem of executive functions: working memory, self-regulation, getting started. Time is felt more than seen, so a deadline only feels real when it's on top of you.

And a 2026 interview study by Chen, Meng and Nie, still a preprint, found something important: when a plan breaks, people feel shame, and they reject tools that feel like surveillance.

Most productivity tools answer with longer lists, streaks and tracking. For this group, that becomes a wall of overdue items and a reason to feel judged.

Cues

  • Say "preprint" out loud. Don't quote prevalence numbers; none are on the slide.

The solution

⏱ 15 sstarts at 0:45ends at 1:00

Open slide 3 →

So we went the other way. One task now. One light on while you do it.

Our version 3 had about twenty features, and we couldn't say which ones anyone would use. So we cut it down to one loop: one thing at a time, a tiny first step, a light instead of a judge, and privacy by default.

4Slide 4 of 15

How it works

⏱ 35 sstarts at 1:00ends at 1:35

Open slide 4 →

Here's the loop. It's literally type, Enter, Enter.

One: I write a task, say "Reply to the landlord", or pick one of today's recommended tasks.

Two: the app proposes a tiny first step: open the message and write only the first sentence. It can be ugly. I can rewrite it. I choose 2, 10 or 25 minutes.

Three: I tap "Light up and start". Foquito lights up and a big, plain timer runs.

Four: when I'm done, the light flies to today on the calendar, and the next task is one tap away.

6Slide 6 of 15

No guilt by design

⏱ 15 sstarts at 1:55ends at 2:10

Open slide 6 →

If I drift away, nothing turns red. If the tab is hidden for 15 seconds, or I don't touch the page for 4 minutes, the light just dims and asks for one tap: "I'm here". Nothing is logged as a failure.

The calendar keeps lights, not streaks. A blank day doesn't erase the others.

And everything stays in the browser. No account, no server, no analytics.

  • Live demo of the nudge: switch tabs, count to twenty, come back.

7Slide 7 of 15

Why it works

⏱ 20 sstarts at 2:10ends at 2:30

Open slide 7 →

Every one of these choices has a "Why?" note inside the app, with links to its sources.

The tiny first step comes from Gollwitzer's implementation intentions and the task-breaking used in Safren's CBT trial in JAMA. Short visible sessions follow Barkley, NICE NG87 and the ADHD-EU practical guide. The no-guilt nudge and the no-streak calendar come from the Chen interviews.

And to be clear: these sources informed the design. None of them evaluates EnFoco. EnFoco is not a treatment.

  • Don't present any of these studies as proof that EnFoco works.

9Slide 9 of 15

Tech: Three.js + Tripo AI 3D

⏱ 15 sstarts at 2:40ends at 2:55

Open slide 9 →

Technically it's a whole world in one HTML file. No build step, static hosting.

Three.js draws the scene. Foquito, the trees, the wooden boards and the rocks are 3D models generated with Tripo AI. They're re-tinted for morning, afternoon and night, and if a model fails to load, a code-built version takes its place. Without WebGL, the same loop runs in plain HTML.

10Vision, Limits & Next Steps · Slide 10 of 15

Vision

⏱ 30 sstarts at 2:55ends at 3:25

Open slide 10 →

Where are we going? Our vision: any adult with ADHD, on any device and in their language, can start the next small thing in under a minute, without shame, surveillance or giving away their data.

Why now: Portuguese experts estimate ADHD affects 1.5 to 3 percent of adults in Portugal, and fewer than one in five are properly diagnosed and treated. Worldwide, about 2.5 percent of adults live with ADHD. The need is large, and most people still lack tools that don't shame them.

We get there in three phases. First, validate with Brainstorm. Then an everyday tool: installable, optional encrypted sync, Spanish. Then evidence and reach: a pre-registered pilot, and ADHD associations in Europe and Latin America.

What doesn't change: one task on screen, no streaks, your data first, and no medical claims without evidence. Every phase answers: did they start, and did they stay?

11Vision, Limits & Next Steps · Slide 11 of 15

Current limitations

⏱ 25 sstarts at 3:25ends at 3:50

Open slide 11 →

Now, honestly, what EnFoco is not, yet.

It isn't validated. We haven't tested it with a single person with ADHD. It isn't a treatment or a medical device.

It's hackathon architecture: one file, no automated tests. Data is local only, so there's no sync between devices. The first step comes from keyword rules, not AI, and the nudge thresholds, 15 seconds and 4 minutes, are our guesses. A running timer is lost if you reload.

We've only tested in Chrome with software rendering, not on low-end phones. The UI is Portuguese and English only. And at night, the wooden boards stay warmer than the scene. Every one of these has a fix on the next slide.

Cues

  • Say the limits plainly and confidently. Being honest here is the strength of this block.

12Vision, Limits & Next Steps · Slide 12 of 15

Next steps

⏱ 25 sstarts at 3:50ends at 4:15

Open slide 12 →

So here's the next year, with a target for every step.

Weeks one to two: harden it. Reload-safe timer, the night tint fix, and tests on real low-end phones. The target is 30 frames per second or an automatic fallback on every device.

Weeks two to six: our first usability round, five to eight adults with ADHD through Brainstorm, with consent. Targets: at least 80% light up within 60 seconds, and a usability score of 70 or more.

Weeks six to ten: tune the nudge from real data, and bring back cut features only if testers ask. Months three to six: PWA, Spanish, encrypted sync, an accessibility audit. Months six to twelve: a pilot protocol with ethics review.

Two north-star metrics guide all of it: Start and Stay. They're measured on the device and shared only with consent. We need Brainstorm's community, clinical advisors, and real devices. And the go-to-market plan is Portugal first, then EU associations.

  • Every number here is a target, not a result. Say "target" or "goal".

Business model

⏱ 20 sstarts at 4:15ends at 4:35

Open slide 13 →

The model is freemium. The daily loop stays free: recommended tasks, the brain dump, the tiny first step, the timers, the light, the Why? notes. No account, and nothing leaves the device.

Premium is proposed, not built: encrypted sync, advanced routines, an AI coach whose suggestions you can always edit, and insights on Start and Stay.

A second proposed channel is B2B and B2B2C: associations like Brainstorm, clinics, schools, employers. We are not stating a price today. A modest subscription is a hypothesis we would validate in product-market fit testing.

After the pitch

Likely questions

Has this been validated?

No. The design comes from public sources. Testing with adults with ADHD is our next step, which is why we want to work with Brainstorm.

Is it a medical tool?

No. It doesn't diagnose or treat, and it doesn't replace professional support.

Does the app watch the user?

No. It only knows whether the tab is visible and whether there was a touch. It doesn't record that, and session time counts anyway.

Where is the data?

Only in the browser, in localStorage. There are buttons to export and delete. The only network requests are for the 3D library and two fonts.

Is there AI?

Not today. The first step comes from keyword rules. If AI is added, it will be opt-in, suggest the first step, and stay editable.

Will it run on a cheap phone?

It's built to be light: few polygons, one light per scene, about 30 fps, and a plain HTML view without WebGL. We haven't tested real low-end devices yet. That's step one on our list.

What did you cut?

Demo personas, motivation boosters, co-focus, energy and mood check-ins, and rhythm-of-the-day charts. They can come back only if testing asks for them.

How will you make money?

Freemium. The daily loop stays free. Premium features — sync, routines, an AI coach, insights — are proposed, and so is a B2B channel with associations, clinics, schools and employers. We are not stating a price today; that is a hypothesis to validate in product-market fit testing, which is why we are asking TESLA Venture Studio for their PMF program.

Why TESLA Venture Studio?

Because the next risk is not "can we build the loop?" — we already have a working prototype. The risk is market: will people with ADHD use it, what should stay free, and how we reach them in Portugal and then the EU. That is exactly the Market Development and Product-Market Fit Program.

And Wexio?

We would like credits to explore a voice assistant that helps someone start out loud. It would be optional, and it would not replace the quiet, local loop we have today.
